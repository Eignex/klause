# 16.1 Witnesses and proof scope

A portfolio is only as sound as what it lets workers share. This subchapter defines
the identity every publication carries, the three outcomes of checking it, and the
extra evidence that bounds, refutations and exhaustion require. Reusable finite and
exact-coordinate checks belong to [source validation](13-validation.md); the
portfolio supplies publication scope and source tolerance policy without owning a
second checker.

## Publication identity

Portfolio evidence names its source model and objective. These are opaque in-memory
identities: a fresh or transformed model needs its own check, even if structurally
equivalent. Source arm materialization installs one immutable `SourceWorkerBinding`
before scheduler admission. It captures model and objective identity, source scope,
the actual producer invocation, a separate containing sharing invocation and proof
authority. Finite worker assumptions may strengthen the sharing scope; those
producer conditions cannot justify unconditional imports into siblings. A completed
binding cannot change or promote witness-only authority, and binding failure closes
the materialized worker before invocation-owned source services unwind in reverse
order.

Every worker kind — finite, linear, polynomial, nonlinear integer and quadratic —
publishes its actual prepared invocation, execution assumptions, owned point and
objective value. Pool admission checks the containing invocation, the point and both
producer and consumer conditions after a native witness check. A model or kind label
cannot replace the checked point, and a shared point keeps its producing publication
instead of acquiring another worker's identity.

## Owned assignments

Open portfolios transport exact assignments in `Sample`: `exactInts` and
`exactReals` carry immutable authoritative coordinates, and finite samples retain
private compact arrays. Constructor inputs and exported arrays cannot mutate a
published incumbent; live producer workspaces remain private (see
[model ownership](03-model.md)). Copying an unchanged assignment preserves its
coordinates, theory acceptance and source certificate. Changing coordinates or
projecting a warm start discards them and requires a fresh witness check. A wide
exact sample exposes no finite integer array; windowed fallback workers project warm
starts that cannot replace exact incumbents or justify bounds.

The exact objective is evaluated from the carried assignment, including integers
outside `Long`. Open affine invocations capture the original owner's exact objective
coefficients and constant once; their exchange orders incumbents by that source value,
separately from the minimized execution value used by worker cutoffs.

## Verification outcomes

Verification preserves three outcomes. Accepted witnesses may become incumbents;
rejected proposals quarantine their producer without proving the model infeasible;
indeterminate checks publish nothing and do not count as a fault. Finite fixpoint
cancellation stays indeterminate. Uncertified real coordinates require a complete
source-row check, including strictness, reification and declared bounds, or the
configured source tolerance check. Unsupported checkers decline. Scoped admission
checks the containing invocation's Boolean pins and numeric windows before the
installed source checker; a numeric real restriction needs exact coordinates.

`OpenTheoryPipeline.execute` and the `searchWithoutTheory` observer overloads publish
`SourceIncumbentPublication` after verified installation, carrying the owned source
sample, exact original-direction `SourceValue` and invocation version. The final
`acceptedIncumbent` retains the latest receipt independently of a terminal's private
proof witness. Callbacks run under the schedule lock; callback failures propagate
after every worker and source service has closed.

## Bounds and certificates

Bounds, infeasibility and unboundedness require a matching completed-proof
certificate in addition to identity. Native adapters issue these certificates only
for their engines' completed proof results; open local search has witness authority
only. A missing certificate cannot justify pruning or a terminal proof, although a
separately accepted point can remain the best incumbent. The certificate is an
in-memory attestation of the engine's check, not a serialized proof; independent
proof export remains a [vision requirement](01-introduction.md#independently-checkable-proof-files).
A point alone never certifies a recession direction.

Objective changes cannot reuse objective bounds or unboundedness claims.
Unconditional variable bounds use the model scope without an objective; a
conditional root floor or integer window cannot prune siblings with weaker
conditions. Completed-result issuance retains the actual `SolveResult` or
`MinimizeResult`, with execution cutoffs in native minimized units separate from
exact original-source claims, which the [derivation verifier](17-evidence.md)
checks.

Affine mixed workers publish checked typed terminals through the same scheduler
hook. After all workers close, the invocation folds their retained, already checked
global root bounds with the shared original-source incumbent. Private branch bounds
and terminals the scheduler did not admit cannot finish the source, and cancellation
retains completed partial bound metadata without promoting an interrupted proof.

## Shared channels

Built-in shared channels belong to the same model and objective. Workers reject
foreign pools; objective and variable bound imports require matching certificates.
The incumbent exchange runs the source witness checker before installing a cutoff,
including for direct engine publications. The low-level public portfolio API still
supports caller-trusted unbound workers with an explicit witness checker; source
pipeline portfolios install the scoped verification contract.

Learned-clause exchange requires at least two materialized backtrack workers,
including replicas on parallel lanes. A lone complete worker keeps its learned
database locally. Shared preparation and incumbent channels retain their own
lifetimes even without a clause peer.

## Exhaustion and metadata

Optimization exhaustion without an incumbent is an original-source refutation only
when the installed complete worker routes objective cutoffs through the scheduler and
used no finite shared cutoff. The scheduler retains observed cutoffs for proof
admission: a callback that returned only positive infinity leaves the source space
unrestricted, and a finite cutoff supports only its conditional bound. With a checked
incumbent, the same unrestricted exhaustion establishes attainment. The completion
still passes the model, objective, invocation and witness checks. Heuristic and
improvement-only arms gain no completion authority from exhaustion.

Ordinary finite verdicts retain the common source interpretation when every worker
is bound to the same source model; an unbound or mixed-source low-level portfolio
cannot infer it. Resolved finite metadata reports complete search only when a
materialized backtrack-family worker permits source proofs and is neither auxiliary
nor improvement-only, so explicit `bt=0` mixes report their actual composition.
Frontends forward that library value without selecting traits from engine names.
This metadata never establishes a verdict or alters proof qualification.

## Theory-backed workers

Workers that need arithmetic completion share the same source scope and require that
check before publishing a witness. A neighbourhood refutation or failed candidate
check cannot establish global infeasibility. Complete workers retain proof authority;
heuristic workers retain witness authority. [Theory contracts](18-theories.md) define
the completion routes and their invocation lifetimes.

MPS finite invocations restrict continuous completion through an immutable numerical
execution policy: LS and ALNS keep their finite recipes and numerical residual LP
completion even when every discrete coordinate is fixed. The configured source
tolerance callback reaches backtrack leaves, LS completion, ALNS bootstrap and CP
repair through the same scenario. Exact MPS invocations supply no tolerance callback,
so a floating completion cannot acquire exact authority through sharing.

Open numerical fallback admits complete numerical workers for BACKTRACK and MIXED.
Each owns its numerical traversal under the captured working-model proof context,
and its typed terminal passes the runtime invocation and source-evidence checks
before the pool can finish. No worker falls back to an arithmetic theory or
materializes a finite domain for an open numerical declaration. Unavailable LS and
ALNS requests return Unsupported before allocating a worker.
