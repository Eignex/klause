# 14.3 Explanations and conflict learning

A deduction is only useful to learning if its reason can be read back later. This
subchapter starts with the reasons propagators record and how long they stay valid,
then follows a conflict through first-UIP analysis, minimization and the two
specialized learning lanes, and ends with how learned constraints are retained and how
restarts and inprocessing use them.

## Explanation lifetime

Propagators supply sound source reasons for deductions and conflicts. An assignment's
undo lifetime can be deeper than the effective level of its reason: Boolean pins use
the deeper of that level and the current decision depth, so an asserting backjump
retains its consequence.

Reasons are built lazily where that is safe. Single-sided linear indicator deductions
record a lazy linear payload during trailed search; conflict analysis and shared
premise export resolve it against the bounds at the pin rather than later domains,
using the undo log to reconstruct historical bounds. Untrailed and two-sided
deductions keep eager explanations. Linear bound propagation constructs a reason only
when the proposed bound tightens the live domain, and reified arithmetic skips reason
construction when a settled relation agrees with its assigned indicator.

Single-variable equality and disequality indicators over root domains contained in
`{0, 1}` propagate as direct channels. Their reifications also watch target
membership: a missing interior target retains its removal premise when pinning the
indicator, because bounds alone cannot justify that conflict. A target absent from the
declared root domain needs no search premise.

Assumption cores retain the complete applied seed prefix when propagation refutes it,
or the full assumption set when a search tree is exhausted. Returned cores are jointly
refuted with the hard model and need not be minimal. Assumption seeding stops before
subsequent pins after cancellation or an interrupted fixpoint, and its applied prefix
cannot certify the full seed. Factor cores traverse the full reason graph with
session-owned scratch and retain the same membership across restarts and
learned-clause compaction.

### Table explanations

Table explanations use build-local caches for historical domains, carve positions and
materialized literals; these caches do not cross propagation or undo. Ground supports
use membership/value-set checks, and support gathering can stop after all live values
of a ground column are covered while every tuple is still checked for feasibility. The
reversible live prefix is committed once per completed sweep; sibling branches restore
filtering from trailed state. Search-time filtering retains STR2 explanations.
Construction-time root propagation folds unary Table restrictions whose expanded
supports total at most 4096 values, so local search samples from the same
unconditional restrictions.

### LP explanations

LP explanations cite selected source bound witnesses and recursively expanded row,
fixing and cutoff premises. Interval reasons exclude the target's own endpoint and
retain all supporting row and bound premises, including the scopes of rows used to
cancel or substitute continuous terms. An unexpressible numerical scope withholds the
consequence. Objective-relative fixing can use a weaker completed certificate while
pruning retains the strongest bound, and keeps its own local reason path.

With LP learning enabled, the bounded, deduplicating LP clause pool admits each
successful row tightening's consequence literal and complete false premises. An
admitted implication registers immediately in the persistent CP session, so it
survives undo and can propagate in sibling branches before restart. These clauses do
not assume an incumbent cutoff. See
[LP consumer contracts](26-lp-architecture.md#consumers-explanations-and-cuts).

## Conflict analysis

`ConflictAnalyzer` walks the implication graph backwards from a conflict, resolving
each step against the antecedents of the resolved variable in reverse assignment order,
until exactly one literal at the current decision level remains: the first unique
implication point. The graph spans Boolean variables and integer order literals
uniformly. Factors emit per-factor conflict reasons and record antecedents on every
force, so the analysis resolves through non-clause deductions instead of treating
them as leaves. A variable with no antecedent is a decision, assumption or root fact.

The analyzer is a driver; the step that folds an antecedent into the accumulating
nogood is delegated to a `ConflictResolvent`. `ClauseResolvent` is the default and the
sound fallback; the pseudo-Boolean resolvent below is the other implementation. The
result is either a learned constraint or not applicable, which happens when the
conflict sits at the root. A learned constraint carries its backjump level (the
second-highest decision level among its literals), its LBD (the number of distinct
decision levels it touches) and whether backjumping makes it assert a literal. The
Boolean and integer variables seen during analysis form the bump set that VSIDS and
CHB consume.

`ClauseMinimizer` shortens every first-UIP clause before it is emitted: first by
self-subsuming resolution, then by binary-resolution minimization. Dropping a
redundant literal always yields a stronger implied clause, so the result stays an
asserting nogood. The minimizer reuses its scratch and redundancy memo within one
analyzer but clears the memo before each conflict, because levels and antecedents
belong to that analysis's frozen reason graph.

Two analyzers share this construction. The native CP analyzer learns over CP reasons
and stores into the native clause database. The shared first-UIP analyzer in
`SearchLearning` learns over component reasons; a shared conflict with usable reasons
asserts and backjumps, while a sound resolvent that cannot assert keeps chronological
fallback. Unexplained component publications cannot supply missing antecedents.

## Native SAT lane

A pure-Boolean model whose factors are all clauses runs on a dedicated lane. The
`nativeSat` setting auto-dispatches by default: the lane is selected exactly when the
propagation projection is eligible, and a custom clause projection makes it
ineligible. Clause literals are packed into an immutable arena whose clause indices
match the model's factors, so reasons still identify source factors, and the arena is
shared read-only across portfolio workers.

Propagation is two-watched-literal unit propagation over a trail pointer, without
per-factor dispatch, an atom store or integer order literals. Each session keeps a
truth byte per Boolean variable for single-load literal checks, mirrored from the
canonical assignment bitsets. Watch positions are rebuilt per session and may drift
across backtracking. Learned clauses live in a growable side buffer; each live learned
clause keeps a replacement-scan cursor that wraps over the complete clause, so undo
can reopen any earlier literal. A learned unit watches its asserting literal and its
deepest false premise. Reductions pack surviving literals and metadata forward in
place, keeping survivor order, watches and cursors aligned. The lane shares the
general trail, pins, antecedents and conflict analyzer, and leaves general CP failure
storage unallocated.

## Pseudo-Boolean learning

On pure-Boolean models, conflict analysis can learn pseudo-Boolean constraints
instead of clauses. Cutting planes refute counting arguments, such as pigeonhole
structure, that resolution needs exponentially many steps for. The `pbLearning`
setting auto-dispatches on for pure-Boolean problems and is ignored when integer
variables are present, because order-literal atoms carry no pseudo-Boolean reason.

The accumulated nogood is a linear `≥` constraint over Boolean literals seeded from
the violated bound. Each resolution step cancels the pivot with the division rule:
the reason is divided by its pivot coefficient, rounding up, then added to the
conflict, which keeps coefficients bounded. Weakening and saturation keep the
constraint canonical. Every step is a nonnegative combination with Chvátal-Gomory
rounding, so the learned constraint is implied by its inputs. When a step cannot
proceed soundly, including when arithmetic would leave the `Long` range, analysis
falls back to clause resolution for that conflict. Learned pseudo-Boolean constraints
are stored alongside clauses and share their retention policy. Private clausal probes
and some portfolio arms disable this learning.

## Learned-clause retention

The native store forgets only when a learned-clause cap is configured. A forgetting
pass then runs at restart boundaries once the database exceeds the cap: permanent
clauses and glue clauses (LBD at most 2 by default) are always kept, and among the
rest the lowest-LBD clauses are kept up to the cap.

With the tiered database enabled, `ClauseTier` replaces that binary split. A clause is
classified by LBD on first encounter: core at or below the glue threshold, mid up to
LBD 6, local above. Core clauses are never deleted. Mid clauses survive reductions but
are demoted to local when idle across one. Local clauses are deleted aggressively but
promoted to mid when they take part in a later conflict. The tier is persistent
per-clause state rather than a function of LBD, so reuse and idleness move clauses
between tiers. The SAT-optimized preset enables a capped tiered database.

The shared learned store used by component search has its cap off by default. When
capped, reduction runs at restart boundaries after pending attachments are drained.
Glue clauses, units and clauses used for propagation since the last reduction survive;
remaining capacity keeps lower LBD first, then older insertion. Protected clauses can
leave the retained count above the nominal cap. See
[SearchSession](../klause/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/eignex/klause/solver/search/SearchSession.kt)
and
[ClauseDb](../klause/src/commonMain/kotlin/com/eignex/klause/backtrack/ClauseDb.kt).

## Restarts, phases and inprocessing

A `RestartSchedule` decides when a run pops back to its root; the restart action
itself (popping the trail, forgetting, inprocessing) stays with the traversal. Each
driver owns its schedule instance. A single unbounded run is the default. The
configurable schedules, in precedence order, are:

- **Mode switching** dives without restarting until a first feasible solution, then
  alternates a stable stage of sparse Luby restarts that dives on the saved phase with
  focused stages of frequent adaptive restarts.
- **EMA adaptive** compares fast and slow exponential moving averages of learned LBD
  and restarts when recent clauses are worse than the long-run average.
- **LBD adaptive** does the same with bounded recent and global windows.
- **Luby** restarts after a Luby multiple of a fixed decision base.

Both adaptive schedules block a restart while the trail is well above its average,
since the search is then driving deep toward a model. Learned constraints, activities
and saved phases survive every restart.

When enabled, `PhaseSaving` keeps the saved polarity of each variable, an optional
target phase (the deepest conflict-free Boolean prefix) and a rephase rotation through
target, saved, all-true, all-false and random polarities every 1000 conflicts by
default. A
mode-switching schedule pins the polarity source to its stage instead.

Inprocessing runs at restart boundaries, with the session at the post-seed root, in
bounded round-robin slices over the learned database. Vivification strengthens a
clause by asserting the negations of its literals under propagation; subsumption drops
learned clauses subsumed by another learned clause and applies self-subsuming
resolution over both the general database and the native arena's learned buffer.
Atom-literal clauses are skipped by vivification.
Every pass must preserve the variable set, because portfolio workers share learned
clauses under stable variable ids and share the native arena read-only; variable
elimination runs once in [presolve](12-presolve.md) instead. Seeded searches skip
inprocessing, since derivations under assumption pins would not hold globally.
