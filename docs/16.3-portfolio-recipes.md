# 16.3 Worker recipes

The previous subchapters apply to every pool. This one describes which workers a
curated pool contains for particular model structures, and how exact arithmetic
sources supply their workers. Each recipe works within the shared scheduling and
publication contract: it changes pool composition, never witness or proof authority.
Explicit pools, backtrack edits and source annotations keep their requested
composition throughout, and containing time, work and LP allowances stay shared.

## Model-specific worker recipes

### Scheduling neighborhood diversity

A curated sequential mixed optimization pool over a scheduling model pairs the
original plateau local-search arm with a primary-repair variant in the preceding
generic CBLS slot. Both are CBLS walks with a stall swap cap of 16 and iterated
local-search restarts (basin hopping over a population of three, improving
acceptance), and both start from the original arm's seed. The variant routes
supplemental Element repairs into a separate alternative list that only complete
improving fallback selection consults; the original arm keeps
its full repair neighborhood. Every other worker keeps its position and seed.
Local-search-only execution, parallel pools and non-scheduling models keep their
selected recipes.

### Annotated finite-domain diversity

A curated single-core mixed optimization pool with at least six configured arms
keeps unrestricted first-fail search beside a source annotation on nonbinary
finite-CP models containing a circuit or subcircuit constraint, when the first
nonempty source tier selects integer first-fail and every declared tier orders values
by minimum. If the pool lacks `first-fail`, composition appends that recipe after the
incumbent workers with ordinary complete proof authority; existing first-fail recipes
are reused without a copy. A tiered first-fail annotation still restricts candidate
scope, so the unrestricted worker adds a distinct branching capability. This adds at
most one complete worker and no family, processor, allowance or reservation.
Boolean-first tiers, other value preferences, satisfaction, binary-domain, noncircuit
and continuous models keep their selected composition.

### Finite objective recipe diversity

A curated single-core mixed finite-CP optimization pool with at least six configured
slots admits the restart-level selector portfolio (`selector-switch`) beside the SAT
guard when its initial backtrack pair has no LP recipe. The worker restarts on a Luby
schedule with base 100 and, at each restart, a UCB1 bandit picks one of four
variable/value pairs: VSIDS with minimum value, dom/wdeg with solution-guided minimum
value, activity-based search with impact values, and conflict ordering over dom/wdeg
with minimum value. A run is rewarded when it finds a solution. The worker occupies
the conflict-driven slot; an expanded pool containing both recipes swaps their
positions. Pool size, local-search positions, slice allowances and family scheduling
are preserved. Continuous models and parallel lanes keep their selected recipes.

### LP arm admission

Curated sequential mixed finite optimization pools add LP-bounded backtrack workers
for three structures. Each keeps existing worker positions and seeds and is skipped
when LP is disabled or unavailable, explicit pools or annotations are present, or
execution is parallel or single-engine. See
[ordinary tableau admission](26-lp-architecture.md).

- **Binary packing.** When every row is a whole unconditional non-strict integer
  capacity inequality with nonnegative signed-word coefficients and at least two
  positively weighted columns, and the minimized objective rewards items with
  nonpositive integer coefficients, the default LP recipe takes the conflict-driven
  slot beside the SAT guard and suppresses a separate aggressive companion.
- **Integer linear models.** Pools with at least six slots over models composed
  entirely of ordinary integer linear rows with an integer objective append an
  aggressive LP companion; pure binary equality models with a nonnegative,
  nonconstant integer objective qualify through exact equality views. The companion
  also runs under a cuts-off ceiling, so separation varies without removing its bound
  search.
- **Continuous columns.** Optimization models with continuous columns rank the
  default LP arm after the SAT guard and conflict-driven core; pools with at least six
  slots append it when the configured backtrack slots lack it. It joins before the
  first incumbent when the objective has a continuous coefficient, and after it
  otherwise. Single-core pools reserve half the scheduled time for it until the first
  incumbent when the objective is continuous and the model is not continuous-majority
  with general integers. After the first incumbent, an auxiliary LP arm without
  progress credit receives a
  [bounded evaluation](16.2-portfolio-scheduling.md#auxiliary-incumbent-evaluation);
  an unproductive trial retires it without claiming exhaustion or decaying its
  siblings' evidence. Configured LP slots never retire under that policy.

### Satisfaction pools

The satisfaction backtrack palette includes conservative LP after default and
aggressive LP; applicability, model class and LP ceilings order and filter it. Root
variable shaving and hull pruning remain explicit plan options, and curated
satisfaction workers harvest root cuts only with an explicit `lp-root-cuts=true`.

The clausal complete-search recipes use clause learning, EMA restarts and a
true-first saved phase, without pseudo-Boolean learning or inprocessing. Their
balanced and stable variants use VSIDS decay 0.975 and 0.99, with database caps of
4000 and 2000. Curated admission requires a clausal model; pseudo-Boolean models keep
their cutting-plane palette. Clausal satisfaction leads its complete slots with the
balanced recipe and, in a curated sequential mixed pool, reserves 98% of active time
for it after the initial probes, because a refutation emits no incumbent progress
from which family rewards could predict it. Automatic single-core mixed clausal
satisfaction uses one local-search and one complete arm. Its local-search slots lead
with fixed-break probSAT, tabu tenure 10 and an improving aspiration rule, without a
strategy restart schedule.

### Boolean objective core search

Curated mixed finite optimization pools append a retained core-guided complete worker
when every objective term is Boolean and the model has no continuous columns. The
worker assumes all positive residual objective terms false and asks for a satisfying
assignment. A refutation returns a core: a set of terms at least one of which must be
violated. The worker adds the core's minimum residual weight to an exact lower bound
and relaxes the core with a reified cardinality, replacing its violation count `s` by
`1 + sum(k = 2..n, s >= k)`; only the first unspent threshold of each count is
materialized. Weights, offsets and the bound use arbitrary-precision integers, and
caller-pinned terms use private selectors so query assumptions cannot overwrite them.

Published incumbents project away auxiliary selectors and pass the source fixpoint
check. Optimality requires exact equality between the original objective and the
accumulated lower bound. Workers whose objective range exceeds exact binary64
integers withhold terminal proof certificates while keeping checked points and
floors. Auxiliary clauses and their learned clauses never enter shared channels.

On a single lane with another complete worker, the core worker joins after the first
checked incumbent once each ordinary complete worker has spent one base of work since
the latest improvement, or after the initial pool retires. It then receives a base
work allowance per turn and at most two seconds of cumulative active time, after
which it returns a budget stop with its floor retained. Parallel pools admit it
immediately, and `core-guided` selects it directly.

## Exact theory strategy execution

Polynomial, nonlinear-integer and linear open mixed invocations, and parallel
single-strategy requests, use the same construction path as finite execution.
Complete-only affine requests supply private complete workers without heuristic
arms. Polynomial workers keep private arithmetic invocations; nonlinear integer
traversal keeps tuple enumeration, candidate state and arithmetic work across slices.
Quadratic finite and open-integer adapters supply retained traversal and exact
neighborhood factories, keeping original and minimized algebraic values separately.
A typed terminal enters the schedule only after source, condition, objective,
authority, witness and exact attainment or strictness checks. Replicas keep private
state and native reservations; FIXED runs one lane.

Supported linear sources pair complete traversal with exact-coordinate local search
and adaptive block repair, including real-only models and arbitrary-width integers.
Neighborhood windows restrict proposals rather than source domains, and neighborhood
refutations carry no source proof. Boolean or continuous linear objectives use
strict-improvement theory rounds when integral descent does not apply; these admit
completed source proofs without guaranteeing termination for every source. Finite
real models with fixed discrete coordinates use the same repair driver, with mixed
products becoming exact linear rows checked against their original relation.

Exact neighborhood handles keep centers, radii, random state and adaptive weights
through slices, and their widening policy handles stalls without reopening the
native allowance. Exact-neighborhood preparation contributes to scheduler instruction
coverage once. A completed sibling can settle a source before another worker runs.
